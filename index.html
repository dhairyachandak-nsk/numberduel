Sun · Snow · Sky — a duel of chains and numbers

How to Play +

The Deck

57 cards total, split across 3 elements — Sun, Snow, and Sky. Each element has the same 19 cards: number cards 1–9 (one of each), plus 10 operator cards — two each of + − ×, and two each of ÷ and bracket { }. Every duel shuffles all 57 and deals the biggest even split possible — 28 cards to each player, 1 left over unused.

Chains

The board has 3 open "chain" slots. A chain is a running math expression built one card at a time. On your turn you play exactly one card, either starting a brand-new chain in an empty slot, or adding to a chain that's already in progress.

The single-element rule: once a chain has its first card, every later card added to it must be the same element. A Snow chain only ever takes more Snow cards. This is also why the game feels like managing three separate lanes — a Sun chain, a Snow chain, and a Sky chain can all be open side by side.

What Counts as a Legal Play

Think of a chain as a sentence that has to stay grammatically valid:

• A chain must start with a number or an opening bracket.
• A number can only follow an operator or an opening bracket (you can't play two numbers in a row).
• An operator (+ − × ÷) can only follow a number or a closed bracket (you can't play two operators in a row).

Brackets Self-Close

There's only one bracket card, and you never "close" it yourself — a bracket automatically snaps shut the instant the expression inside it becomes a computable result on its own. Example: play {, then 2, then +, then 3 — the moment that 3 lands, the bracket closes itself into (2+3) automatically, worth 5 as a single value in the outer chain.

Finishing a Chain

A chain scores and clears the instant it becomes a complete, computable expression — but a single lone number by itself does not count. You need at least one full operation (a number, an operator, and another number) before a chain can resolve. Whoever plays the card that completes it scores the result, rounded to two decimal places, and the slot opens back up for a new chain.

Star Cards (★)

One ÷ and one bracket { } per element are marked with a star. Playing a star card doesn't end your turn — you immediately get a free follow-up play of a number card onto that same chain, no extra turn needed. That bonus play runs on its own fixed 15-second clock (see below).

Dividing by Zero

Not allowed. If a ÷ card is on top of a chain, you can't play a 0 right after it — the game will simply block the move.

Turn Timer

Choose 15, 30, 45, or 60 seconds before the duel starts — that's how long each player gets per turn. Let the clock hit zero and your turn is forfeited to your opponent, no card played. The one exception is the star-card bonus play, which always gets its own fixed 15 seconds regardless of what you picked for the main timer.

End of the Duel

The game ends the moment it becomes a player's turn and they have no legal move anywhere — no card in hand fits any open chain, and no card can start a new one (or all 3 slots are already full of chains they can't extend). Scores are tallied and whoever has more points wins.
