NUMBERDUEL

Sun · Snow · Sky — a duel of chains and numbers

Begin the Duel

How to Play +

The Deck

57 cards total, split across 3 elements — Sun, Snow, and Sky. Each element has the same 19 cards: number cards 1–9 (one of each), plus 10 operator cards — two each of + − ×, and two each of ÷ and bracket { }. Every duel shuffles all 57 and deals the biggest even split possible — 28 cards to each player, 1 left over unused.

Chains

The board has 3 open "chain" slots. A chain is a running math expression built one card at a time. On your turn you play exactly one card, either starting a brand-new chain in an empty slot, or adding to a chain that's already in progress.

The single-element rule: once a chain has its first card, every later card added to it must be the same element. A Snow chain only ever takes more Snow cards. This is also why the game feels like managing three separate lanes — a Sun chain, a Snow chain, and a Sky chain can all be open side by side.

Opponent: 2 Players or AI

Play locally against a friend, or switch to vs AI on the setup screen for a single-player practice opponent. The AI always plays second, its hand stays hidden (you'll just see face-down cards and a "Thinking…" message). Pick Easy for a casual opponent that just grabs the best move available right now, or Hard for one that looks several moves ahead and guesses at your hidden hand from the cards it hasn't seen yet — it never peeks at your cards, but expect a tough fight.

What Counts as a Legal Play

Think of a chain as a sentence that has to stay grammatically valid:

• A chain must start with a number or an opening bracket.
• A number can only follow an operator or an opening bracket (you can't play two numbers in a row).
• An operator (+ − × ÷) can only follow a number or a closed bracket (you can't play two operators in a row).

Confirming a Play

Selecting a card and clicking a chain doesn't commit it right away — you'll see a ghost preview of the card in that slot plus Confirm/Cancel buttons, so a misclick can't lock in a move you didn't mean to make. The turn timer, if it's on, keeps running the whole time.

Brackets Self-Close

There's only one bracket card, and you never "close" it yourself — a bracket automatically snaps shut the instant the expression inside it becomes a computable result on its own. Example: play {, then 2, then +, then 3 — the moment that 3 lands, the bracket closes itself into (2+3) automatically, worth 5 as a single value in the outer chain.

Finishing a Chain

A chain scores and clears the instant it becomes a complete, computable expression — but a single lone number by itself does not count. You need at least one full operation (a number, an operator, and another number) before a chain can resolve. Whoever plays the card that completes it scores the result, rounded to two decimal places, and the slot opens back up for a new chain.

Star Cards (★)

One ÷ and one − per element are marked with a star. Playing a star card doesn't end your turn — you immediately get a free follow-up play of a number card onto that same chain, no extra turn needed. If the turn timer is on, that bonus play always runs on its own fixed 15-second clock, separate from your main turn timer.

Dividing by Zero

Not allowed. If a ÷ card is on top of a chain, you can't play a 0 right after it — the game will simply block the move.

Turn Timer (Optional)

Off by default — Numberduel is untimed unless you turn this on. Switch it on and pick 15, 30, 45, or 60 seconds per turn; let the clock hit zero and your turn is forfeited to your opponent, no card played. You also pick a time-out penalty (−5, −10, −15, or −30 points), deducted from whoever let the clock run out — on either a main turn or a star-card bonus — specifically to discourage stalling on purpose to dodge a bad position. See the Warning note under the timer settings for more on that.

End of the Duel

The game ends the moment it becomes a player's turn and they have no legal move anywhere — no card in hand fits any open chain, and no card can start a new one (or all 3 slots are already full of chains they can't extend). Scores are tallied (penalties and all) and whoever has more points wins — along with a quick breakdown of the match's biggest chain, most-used element, average chain value, and longest bracket expression.

Warning +

Turning on a turn timer opens the door to a strategic exploit: a player stuck with no good move can simply let the clock run out on purpose instead of playing a bad card, effectively "passing" for free. The time-out penalty above exists specifically to discourage this — every time-out, whether on a main turn or a star-card bonus, docks that player points (scores can go negative). Pick a penalty steep enough to make stalling not worth it, or just leave the timer off entirely for relaxed, untimed play.
